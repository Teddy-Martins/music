Lesson three

Why some notes sound right together

Theory isn't a separate subject from playing - it's a short explanation for patterns your ear already notices. Three ideas cover most of what you need early on.

The C major scale

Play every white key from C to the next C. That's the C major scale - seven notes, no sharps or flats, built from a fixed pattern of whole and half steps: W-W-H-W-W-W-H.

Intervals

An interval is the distance between two notes. A few are worth knowing by ear immediately.

Interval Example Sounds like
Major 3rd C to E Bright, "happy" - the sound of a major chord
Minor 3rd C to E♭ Darker, "sad" - the sound of a minor chord
Perfect 5th C to G Open and stable - the backbone of most chords
Octave C to C Same note, higher pitch

Key signatures

A key signature tells you which notes are consistently sharp or flat throughout a piece, so they don't need to be marked every time. C major has none - it's the easiest place to start, and every other key is a variation on the same seven-note pattern, shifted to a new starting note.
